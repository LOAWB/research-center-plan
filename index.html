01The read

The product is ready. The distribution does not exist yet.

Research Center sells an annual membership into Louis Bachour's real portfolio — every entry, exit, position size and the reasoning behind it, with a private Telegram community and in-person member events. The trade history has been time-stamped in a public Telegram channel since 20 March 2021.

That is a genuinely strong asset, and the offer around it is already well built: a qualifying quiz that disqualifies the wrong buyer on risk appetite, net worth and budget, a VSL, testimonial video, and a disclaimer set that is more careful than most of the category.

What is missing is everything that turns an offer into a business. There is no audience, no tracking, no paid media, and no free front end — and in this category the free front end is the acquisition engine.

It is a recruitment problem, not a product problem.

And the industry spends heavily to get there

MarketWise, the listed parent of Stansberry Research and Chaikin Analytics, is the only public company in the category. FY2025: $271.2M billings, $328.1M net revenue, and $131.0M of sales and marketing — 40% of revenue, or 48% of billings, depending which denominator you use. It has run at 39–44% of revenue every year on record. Any budget proposed below sits under that benchmark on either measure.

The more useful disclosure is what they are doing with it. Paid subscribers fell from 506,000 to 374,000 in a year — down 26% — while reported ARPU rose from $394 to $670. Management says the price increases were deliberate and that subscriber count has become "a less meaningful driver." The share of subscribers who have spent over $5,000 lifetime went from 25% to 30%.

That is the whole industry moving toward the model Research Center already is: fewer, better-qualified, higher-paying members. The application gate that looks like a constraint is the direction the category's largest operator is spending years and a shrinking subscriber file to reach.

02Blocker

Nothing on the site is being tracked

Verified · blocking

Day-one blocker

A headless-browser audit of researchcenter.group on 2026-09-09 found no analytics or advertising pixel of any kind. fbq, gtag, dataLayer and ttq are all undefined, and no request is made to facebook.com/tr, connect.facebook.net, Google Tag Manager, Google Analytics, TikTok, Bing or Clarity.

Consequences, in order of cost: Meta cannot optimise toward a conversion, so every campaign would run blind on link clicks. No retargeting audience exists or has ever been accumulating — the most profitable audience in a high-ticket funnel is simply absent. And there is no attribution, so spend cannot be judged.

The site runs on GoHighLevel (leadconnectorhq.com) with Vidalytics hosting the VSL and three testimonial videos, behind Cloudflare. That is good news: GoHighLevel already carries forms, scheduling, email, SMS and pipeline, so the whole nurture and booking apparatus in this plan can be built where the site already lives, without new infrastructure.

Fix before any spend

  • Meta pixel plus Conversions API. Browser-only pixels lose a large share of iOS signal; a high-ticket funnel with few conversions cannot afford that loss. Server-side events from GoHighLevel with deduplication.
  • Define the conversion events properly. Registered, Attended, CallBooked, CallHeld, Purchase with value. Optimise campaigns toward registration at first — at 40 sales a month there is nowhere near enough volume to train delivery on purchases — then move to Attended or CallBooked once weekly volume supports it.
  • Send offline conversions back. Closes happen on a call, so the sale never touches the website. Push closed-won back to Meta with the click identifier or Meta will never learn which leads were worth buying.
  • GA4 and a call-booking source field so booked calls can be traced to campaign and creative.

06Position

The wedge is the timestamp, not the return

The category's competitors anchor on three things Louis cannot credibly claim: assets under management (Capitalist Exploits leads with "Copy a $360M Fund"), institutional pedigree (hedge fund manager, "60-year Wall Street legend"), or sheer signal volume (Hedgeye's 6,800+ trade signals).

He has something none of them lead with, and it happens to be the most defensible asset in a category with a credibility problem:

A single operator's own money, in one portfolio, with every position posted publicly and time-stamped before the outcome was known, continuously since March 2021 — and a return figure modest enough to be believed.

The ~30% a year is a marketing asset precisely because it is not spectacular. A buyer with $500,000 has been pitched 1,000% option trades for years and has learned to discount them entirely. A compounding figure in the high twenties, presented with its own caveats attached, reads as real. It should be stated plainly and never inflated — inflating it would trade the one genuine advantage for the thing everyone else has.

Retire this framing
  • "Millions of dollars generated" — unverifiable, and reads like every guru ad
  • "Join a team that's already winning" — no information in it
  • Any headline number without its timeframe attached
Lead with this instead
  • Every position, time-stamped in public before the outcome — since March 2021
  • The whole portfolio, including the losers, not a highlight reel
  • One operator, his own capital, concentrated and disclosed

"The whole portfolio, including the losers" is the line no competitor in this sweep is willing to write, and it is the one Research Center can actually stand behind because the Telegram archive already proves it.

Which playbook to copy

The category runs on two incompatible proof mechanisms. The Agora and Stansberry lineage sells through dramatic, largely unverifiable claims wrapped in fine-print disclaimers. The Motley Fool sells through a long-running, dated, published scorecard. Research Center must build on the second and stay away from the first, and the reason is structural rather than moral.

A publishing house can put an anonymous "the editors of X Research" byline on a fear-led promo, and when it goes wrong it winds the brand down — as MarketWise did with Legacy Research in February 2024, citing reputational harm from former employees' misconduct. Louis has no such firewall. Every claim traces to one named person who intends to still be selling under that name in ten years. The asset he is building is the archive, and the archive is only worth something if nothing attached to it ever needs disowning.

Three additions that pay for themselves

  • A 30-day guarantee. The category standard is 30 to 90 days. On an unaudited track record at a four-figure price, this is the single highest-leverage conversion change available, and the refund exposure is bounded by the application gate already filtering out the wrong buyer.
  • Payment plans at checkout. Commas, the processor already in place, carries nine BNPL providers and financing from $30 to $465,000, with the seller paid in full upfront. On a $7,500 tier this converts the price objection directly.
  • Real enrollment windows. The quiz already says spots are limited each quarter. Macro Ops genuinely opens and closes enrollment, and closed periods build the list for the next open. Make the scarcity true, then use it.
  • Credit-back on the upgrade. Stansberry's published prospectus credits 100% of a subscriber's prior spend against its top-tier price. Applied here: a member's paid $3,600 comes straight off the Partner tier. It costs nothing on a first-year upgrade, it makes the upgrade conversation trivially easy, and it is the one mechanic from the high-ticket playbook that transfers to a solo operator without any of the risk attached to the rest of it.
  • Keep cancellation frictionless, and say so. The loudest public complaints in this category are not about stock picks — they are about billing. The most-criticised operator in the sweep gates its cancel button behind a video you cannot skip. Research Center already lets members cancel from the Commas receipt email with no account needed. That is a real trust advantage sitting unused; put it on the sales page.
One caution on the price. The $14k and $33k tiers in this category are justified by a value stack — you are buying twenty newsletters for the price of two. Research Center has one product, so the same argument is unavailable. The $3,600 and $7,500 have to be carried by depth of access: the full book rather than a sleeve, the reasoning rather than the signal, the room and the events rather than a PDF. If the sales page argues volume, it loses to a $499 competitor. If it argues access, it has no competitor.
08Funnel

The webinar is the funnel

Cold traffic goes to a free webinar registration, not to the application. The webinar does the teaching, the proving and the qualifying in one 70-minute sitting, and only people who sit through it are asked for anything.

This is the category's own proven shape, not an import: Macro Ops runs a free Trading & Investing Masterclass into its cohort enrollment, Hedgeye runs a free 90-minute masterclass, Chaikin runs live "Urgent Briefing" sessions as its VSL, and Warrior Trading's entire front end is free webinar registration.

What the ad data does and does not support

The claims above describe how these competitors build their funnels on their own sites. Sweep #2 measured something narrower and harder: what they buy cold Meta traffic into. Zero of the 242 confirmed competitor ads drive to a webinar registration. The only CTAs in the entire set are "Learn more" and "See details." What is demonstrably proven in paid traffic in this category is the free email opt-in and the $1 tripwire, one of which has run for 399 days.

That does not make the webinar wrong. Nobody in the sample is testing it, so it is untested here rather than disproven, and the in-room checkout and booking that Commas provides are a genuine structural advantage the others do not have. But it does mean the funnel below rests on assumed rates with no direct paid-media precedent in this category. Prove it at low spend before scaling, and treat the $1 tripwire as the identified fallback if cost per registration or show rate comes in badly.

The stack already does this. Commas — the processor Research Center is already on — runs webinars with checkout inside the room, nine BNPL providers at that checkout, and, for offers that close on a call, in-room calendar booking so an attendee never leaves the session. Nothing new needs buying to run this.

What the webinar actually is

The temptation is a prediction webinar — the coming crash, the one stock. That is the Agora playbook and section 06 already ruled it out. Build the other kind: a method webinar with the archive as the evidence.

Working structure, roughly 70 minutes: how Louis decides what to own and how much of it; the three rules and what each one has cost him; then the proof pass — open the actual Telegram channel on screen and walk two or three real positions from the original timestamped message through to the exit, including one that lost money. Then the offer, then live Q&A. The losing trade is not a concession, it is the most persuasive ten minutes in the session and the reason the FTC exposure in section 11 stays low: nobody walks out of that webinar believing returns are guaranteed.

Live first, evergreen second

Run it live and weekly for the first eight to twelve weeks. Live is where the deck gets fixed, where the objection library gets written from real Q&A, and where show rates are highest. Only once the live version converts should it be recorded and run just-in-time — evergreen a webinar that has not yet proven itself and you scale a session that does not work.

Two economics notes. The webinar compresses the sales cycle — an attendee has already had 70 minutes of Louis before the call, which is why the close rate here is set above the cold-application case. And it front-loads the work: one asset, iterated weekly, carries the entire funnel, which suits a solo operator far better than a large library of content that all has to keep performing.

09Traffic

Where the 5,600 monthly leads come from

Paid — the volume, from month one

Meta is the primary channel. Advantage+ audiences with a broad-ish target and creative doing the qualifying: the ad copy itself should mention a portfolio, a net worth bracket, or a decade of investing so the wrong buyer self-selects out before the click. Optimise to the opt-in first, then to the application once volume supports it.

YouTube in-stream and in-feed second, from month three. This is where the category's creator-led operators actually built their audiences, and Louis's material — long-form macro reasoning — is native to it.

Newsletter sponsorships third. Direct placements in adjacent finance newsletters buy pre-qualified readers at a known price without any platform-policy risk at all. Test three placements in month four.

Organic — the margin, compounding from month one

The Substack has 193 subscribers and has not published since 19 August 2024. The archive is good: real macro reasoning, a distinct voice, and a natural instinct for teasing rather than dumping. Revive it as The Letter in week one and publish weekly without exception.

Under a webinar-first funnel the Letter is not the front door — it is the tail, and the tail is where most of the revenue is. Every registrant who no-shows, watches without buying, or books and does not close lands on it, and gets re-invited to a later session. A webinar converts a small fraction of a room on the day; the list converts the rest over the following months. Treat registrations as list-building that happens to also sell, and the media budget starts making sense at a much lower same-day conversion rate.

The content nobody else can make

The Telegram archive is a five-and-a-half-year, time-stamped, publicly verifiable record — and it is sitting unused as marketing material. Every past position is a finished content unit:

  • The retrospective series. "In March 2022 I posted this. Here is the message, here is the timestamp, here is what happened, and here is what I got wrong." Screen-record the actual channel. It cannot be faked and no competitor can answer it.
  • The losing trades, deliberately. A monthly post on a position that went against him, with the original reasoning intact. This is the highest-trust content available to this business and the cheapest to produce.
  • Live position teardowns. One current holding, the full thesis, the sizing logic, the exit conditions — given away free. This is the Chaikin mechanic executed honestly.

Cadence: one long-form video and one Letter per week, cut into three to five short clips each for Reels, Shorts and X. That is a realistic solo load and it feeds every paid channel with proven creative.

Seed other people's audiences before buying strangers'

The most capital-efficient growth lever found anywhere in this sweep was not advertising. Blossom Social attributes roughly a third of its user growth to a managed roster of finance creators, and Qualtrim was built almost entirely on its founder sponsoring his own videos plus riding an existing creator-sponsorship network. For an operator starting from 1,148 followers, paying or partnering with mid-sized finance creators to put Louis in front of an audience that already trusts someone is cheaper per qualified lead than cold Meta traffic, and it compounds into the owned list. Budget for it from month two.

A risk this plan creates

Running a large free content engine alongside a paid portfolio invites one specific attack, and it is the one that has actually damaged operators in this category: a critic lines up the free commentary against the paid positions and publishes the contradictions. The most-criticised competitor in this sweep was taken apart precisely this way — recommending a sector on the paid side that its free videos were calling bearish the same week.

The operating rule that prevents it: the free content and the paid book must always be reconcilable by a motivated stranger. If Louis is bearish on the Letter, the portfolio should show it, or the Letter should explain why it does not. And the Telegram archive must never be pruned — a track record that is quietly trimmed when it goes bad is worth less than no track record at all, and it would destroy the single asset this entire plan is built on.

10Creative

Six angles, and the rules they all obey

Section 13 schedules six angles across three formats for weeks 4 to 8. These are those six. Each one is built from something Research Center can actually prove, and each is checked against the platform and regulatory constraints in section 11 before it is written, not after it is rejected.

Every ad here sells one thing: a seat at the free webinar. Not the membership, not a call, not the Letter. The ad's only job is a registration.

The four rules, non-negotiable

1. Never state or imply the reader's outcome. Louis showing his own trades is the product and is legitimate. Copy that lets a reader infer his results will be theirs is the precise act prosecuted in FTC v. Warrior Trading. Describe the access, never the outcome.

2. Telegram never appears. Meta prohibits investment ads suggesting interaction via direct messaging, and Telegram is the delivery mechanism. The archive can be shown on screen as evidence without ever being named as the channel you join.

3. Every performance figure carries its date, its source and its caveat. The ~30%/yr is self-calculated and unaudited. That sentence travels with the number, always, at readable size.

4. No manufactured urgency. The quarterly enrollment window is real and may be used. Nothing invented may.

Why these six and not the category's usual six

The live ads split into two registers. The one to avoid is the Agora-lineage curiosity gap, captured running this week: Brownstone's claim a stake in this digital tollbooth for as little as $2, Behind the Markets' the next energy giant is hiding in plain sight, Chaikin's the unseen winner of the AI race is not SpaceX. Each conceals a stock name behind an implied outcome and a deadline. It works, and it is exactly the shape that has drawn the SEC, the FTC and two state AGs onto Agora. Louis has no brand to disown if it goes wrong.

The register to build in is the one the two most durable advertisers in the study are actually running: the Fool's Judge Us by the Scorecard, and the plain, claim-free free-email opt-in that has run for 399 days. Research Center's asset, a five-and-a-half-year timestamped public record that includes the losses, is built for the second register and cannot compete in the first.

The six

ANGLE 1 · THE TIMESTAMP Every position I have taken since March 2021 was posted in public before anyone knew how it would turn out. The entry. The size. The reasoning. Then whatever actually happened, good or bad, on the same public record.

Most people selling research show you the wins after the fact. That is not proof of anything. Proof is a timestamp you could have checked at the time. The claim only Louis can make. Competitors anchor on AUM, pedigree or signal volume because most have no falsifiable public record. A buyer with $500k discounts unverifiable results automatically. A record that could have been checked at the time survives that discount. Best format: screen recording of the archive scrolling with dates visible. Costs nothing and cannot be faked.
ANGLE 2 · THE LOSING TRADE Here is a trade I got wrong.

I posted the entry publicly, I posted the reasoning, and the reasoning did not hold. It is still on the record, with the date on it, because taking it down would make everything else on that record worthless.

If you are looking for someone who only wins, I am not it and I would not believe anyone who claimed to be. The strongest angle available, and the one no competitor will run. It does three jobs at once: it proves the archive is unedited, which retroactively validates every winner in it; it repels the jackpot buyer, who is the wrong buyer and the expensive one; and it is the best available protection against the Warrior Trading exposure, because nobody who watches Louis walk through a loss can claim he led them to expect a guaranteed outcome. Requires Louis on camera.
ANGLE 3 · THE POSITION SIZE A stock tip tells you nothing.

Two people can buy the same company on the same day and one of them is fine and one of them is in trouble, and the only difference is how much of the portfolio went in.

Nobody in this business publishes that number. I publish mine, on every position, at the time I take it. The gap in the entire category. Everyone sells what to buy; almost nobody discloses how much of their own money went in. It speaks over the beginner's head straight to the qualified buyer, which is the filtering the ad has to do anyway. Someone who cares about sizing already has a portfolio, which is the quiz's disqualifier applied earlier and for free. Guardrail: keep it descriptive of what Louis does, never prescriptive about the reader, which is the line the publisher's exclusion turns on.
ANGLE 4 · THE MODEST NUMBER I am not going to tell you I made 1,000% on anything.

The portfolio has compounded at roughly 30% a year since March 2021. I calculated that myself from the public record and it has not been independently audited, so treat it as my own figure rather than a verified one.

I would rather tell you that than round it up. The number's believability is the asset. Against a feed of triple-digit claims, a high-twenties compounding figure with its own disclaimer attached reads as the only honest thing on the page. The caveat is the creative, so it must be said aloud rather than buried in fine print. Highest regulatory load of the six. This is the one counsel should read first, and it should not run until they have.
ANGLE 5 · WHAT I DO NOT DO This is probably not for you.

I do not trade options. I do not day trade. I do not send alerts telling you to buy something in the next ten minutes. If you are looking to turn a small account into a large one quickly, nothing I do will help you and you will be annoyed by how slow it is.

What I do is run one concentrated portfolio, post every position publicly with the reasoning, and hold things for a long time. Disqualification as the hook. The quiz already rejects the wrong buyer; doing it in the ad is free, whereas doing it after the click is not. Simpler Trading is running this register live. Naming who it is not for raises intent among everyone who stays, and keeps the wrong applicant off the calendar. Guardrail: stays self-descriptive. The moment it becomes "unlike the gurus who lie to you" it is a comparative claim about identifiable competitors, which is a different and worse risk.
ANGLE 6 · THE METHOD, NOT THE PREDICTION I am not going to tell you what to buy this week.

What I will do is show you the three rules I use to decide whether anything is worth owning, and what each of those rules has cost me when I ignored it.

Then I will open the public record and walk two positions from the original post through to the exit. One of them worked. One of them did not.

About seventy minutes, free, and there is a live Q&A at the end. The angle that matches the room. Section 08 specifies a method webinar and rules out a prediction webinar, so the ad has to promise what the room delivers. A registrant expecting a hot pick who gets a decision framework is a no-show next week and a refund the week after. This angle protects the funnel's weakest number, which is the show rate.

How to test them

Do not launch six angles at once. At the week 4 to 8 budget, six angles across three formats splits the spend eighteen ways and nothing gathers enough registrations to read.

  • Wave 1: angles 1, 2 and 6. The archive, the loss and the method. They match the webinar most directly and carry the lowest compliance load. One format each, the cheapest to produce, which is the screen recording.
  • Wave 2: the two best formats of whatever survived, plus angle 5, which is cheap to make and filters hard.
  • Wave 3: angle 4 once counsel has cleared the number, and angle 3 once there is evidence the audience is qualified rather than beginners.

Read cost per registration first, then show rate by angle, which is where the real difference will appear. An angle can win on cost per registration and lose badly on attendance, and attendance is what this funnel runs on.

None of this is measurable yet

Every number above is unreadable until the pixel and Conversions API are installed. The tracking implementation is specified in full in the blocker section and is not yet assigned to anyone. Until it is done, no angle can be judged and no spend should start.

11Compliance

What may and may not be said

Primary sources · fetched 2026-09-09

Highest-probability cause of rejection

Meta's Financial and Insurance Products and Services policy explicitly prohibits ads that promote investment products or opportunities that suggest user interaction with the advertiser via on-platform or off-platform direct messaging services.

Research Center's entire delivery mechanism is a Telegram channel, and its own terms instruct members to message Louis on Telegram to cancel. Ads must never present Telegram, DMs or "message me" as the mechanism. Sell the research, the letter and the portfolio; the delivery channel stays off the ad and off the landing page above the fold.

Prosecuted precedent against a near-identical business

In April 2022 the FTC sued Warrior Trading and its founder Ross Cameron for marketing that showcased the founder's own trading results in a way that implied customers would achieve similar outcomes, when most customers lost money. It settled for $3M, and in January 2023 the FTC refunded $2.9M to 20,402 consumers.

That is the same buyer, the same category, and structurally the same claim Research Center's product is built on. Louis showing his own trades is the product and is entirely legitimate. What is not legitimate is any copy that lets a reader infer his results are what they will get. The line runs through the marketing, not the research: describe the access, never the outcome. Every performance figure needs a date, a source, and a plain statement that member results will differ.

FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) apply to the testimonial videos already on the page: any testimonial implying a financial result needs substantiation and a clear not-typical disclosure, and any incentive given for it must be disclosed.

The same policy warns that advertisers may be required to be licensed in the country they are targeting and that Meta may review licensing. Meta's Unacceptable Business Practices policy separately bans deceptive or exaggerated claims about the success of a product or service and names investment opportunities as a category where it commonly sees violations and may demand additional verification.

Google's financial products policy requires the destination page to carry the business's physical address, all associated fees, and links to any third-party accreditation claimed. The Wyoming address is already in the footer; fees are not disclosed anywhere on the landing page, only as budget bands inside the quiz. That must be fixed before any Google spend. Google gives a 7-day warning before suspension.

Google also gates the account itself: US financial advertisers must clear the Advertiser Verification Program — identity verification, business-operations verification, then any policy-specific certification — and those requirements were expanded again in June 2026. This is days to weeks of lead time, not a same-day switch. Start it in week one alongside the Meta business verification, or it becomes the thing that holds up launch.

Will get the account banned
  • Any guaranteed, promised or "risk-free" return
  • Income claims implying typical member results
  • "Join my Telegram" as the ad's call to action
  • Screenshots of account balances as the primary proof
  • Urgency invented for the ad ("3 spots left today")
  • Any crypto framing — that needs written permission plus a licence
Defensible, and already true
  • "Every position time-stamped in public since March 2021"
  • The ~30%/yr figure stated with its own disclaimer attached
  • "Not investment advice. Not a broker-dealer or adviser."
  • Testimonials with a clear not-typical disclosure
  • Free educational research as the offer in the ad
  • The full risk disclosure already on the site

On the publisher's exclusion

The terms rely on the publisher's exclusion under §202(a)(11)(D) of the Investment Advisers Act, which covers general, impersonal commentary distributed to all subscribers alike. The commercial risk is that the sales call and the private community are exactly where that line gets crossed. A closer who tells an applicant what they should buy given their portfolio has just given personalised advice. The call script needs a hard rule against individualised recommendations, and it needs it before the first call. This is a lawyer question, not a marketing question, and it should go to one.
